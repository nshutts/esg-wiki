# CSRD and ESRS

The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) is the EU's mandatory sustainability reporting law, which includes technical standards known as ESRS. This framework requires comprehensive reporting, focusing on double materiality, and applies to large EU companies and certain non-EU groups.

## Key details

- CSRD components include double materiality (impacts on people and planet, plus financial risk), 2 cross-cutting and 10 topical ESRS, mandatory limited assurance, and digital tagging (now postponed). ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- The revised ESRS adopted on 3 July 2026 cut mandatory datapoints by over 60% and total datapoints by over 70% (European Commission). ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- In scope for CSRD are EU companies with more than 1,000 employees and more than EUR 450m net turnover. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- Non-EU groups are in scope with over EUR 450m EU turnover and an EU subsidiary or branch above EUR 200m (Latham & Watkins). ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- The CSRD is mandatory for large EU companies, with a phased approach starting in 2024. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, CSRD row]])
- The CSRD aligns with the European Green Deal and GRI, and expands on NFRD by integrating double materiality (financial + sustainability impact). ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, CSRD row]])
- Enforcement involves penalties set by each Member State, sign-off by statutory auditors or accredited assurers, and supervision by national market regulators for listed issuers. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
