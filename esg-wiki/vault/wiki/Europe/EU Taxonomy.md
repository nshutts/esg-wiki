# EU Taxonomy

The EU Taxonomy is a classification system that defines which economic activities are considered environmentally sustainable. It is important because companies report the share of turnover, CapEx, and OpEx that is eligible and aligned with these objectives.

## Key details

- It is a classification system defining which economic activities count as environmentally sustainable. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- Components include six environmental objectives; an activity is "aligned" if it substantially contributes to one, does no significant harm to the others and meets minimum social safeguards. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- Companies report the share of turnover, CapEx and OpEx that is eligible and aligned. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- Since January 2026, activities below 10% of a KPI can be excluded from detailed assessment and templates are about 64% shorter (BCLP). ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- In scope are companies subject to CSRD (Article 8), plus financial market participants through SFDR. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- Enforcement is through the CSRD and SFDR supervisory regimes. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])

## Related notes

- [[CSRD and ESRS]]: Companies in CSRD scope report Taxonomy KPIs under Article 8.
- [[SFDR]]: Financial market participants are brought into the Taxonomy through SFDR.
