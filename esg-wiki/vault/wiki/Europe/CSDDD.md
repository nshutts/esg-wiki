# CSDDD

The Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) imposes a conduct obligation on companies to identify, prevent, and remediate adverse human rights and environmental impacts in their operations and supply chains. This directive applies to large EU and non-EU companies, requiring compliance starting from July 26, 2029.

## Key details

- CSDDD is a conduct obligation, not just a disclosure one. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=3|Briefing p.3]])
- Components of CSDDD include identifying, preventing, and remediating adverse human rights and environmental impacts in the company's own operations and chain of activities, with a risk-based focus. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=3|Briefing p.3]])
- In scope are EU companies with more than 5,000 employees and EUR 1.5bn net worldwide turnover, and non-EU companies with EUR 1.5bn EU turnover. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=3|Briefing p.3]])
- Compliance is required from 26 July 2029. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=3|Briefing p.3]])
- Enforcement involves national supervisory authorities with administrative fines; the Omnibus narrowed the liability and penalty provisions. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=3|Briefing p.3]])

## Related notes

- [[Omnibus I Directive]]: The Omnibus narrowed CSDDD's liability and penalty provisions.
- [[CSRD and ESRS]]: CSDDD is a conduct obligation, while CSRD is a disclosure obligation.
