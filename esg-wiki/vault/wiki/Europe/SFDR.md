# SFDR

The Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR) sets disclosure rules for asset managers, insurers, pension funds, and advisers in the EU. It mandates specific product labels based on ESG promotion or sustainable objectives, and SFDR 2.0 is planned to replace the current structure.

## Key details

- SFDR disclosure rules apply to asset managers, insurers, pension funds, and advisers. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing pp.2-3]])
- Components of SFDR include entity-level principal adverse impact statements and product labels under Article 8 ("promotes" ESG) and Article 9 (sustainable objective). ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing pp.2-3]])
- SFDR 2.0 is expected to replace the current structure with three categories: Sustainable, Transition, and ESG Basics. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing pp.2-3]])
- Each of the three SFDR 2.0 categories requires 70% of assets to meet the category's criteria. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing pp.2-3]])
- SFDR 2.0 Council position was agreed upon in June 2026, and Parliament's committee position in September 2026; trilogues are expected to start in October. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing pp.2-3]])
- SFDR is in scope for financial market participants and advisers marketing products in the EU, including non-EU managers. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing pp.2-3]])
- Enforcement involves national financial regulators with ESMA coordinating, and greenwashing in fund names and marketing is an active enforcement priority. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing pp.2-3]])
