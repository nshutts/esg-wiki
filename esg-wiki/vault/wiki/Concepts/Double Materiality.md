# Double Materiality

Double materiality is an emphasis in sustainability reporting, particularly within the CSRD, that integrates both financial materiality (impacts on financial performance) and impact materiality (impacts on people and planet). This approach is a key feature of the CSRD and is integrated into standards like the ISSB.

## Key details

- CSRD integrates double materiality, which includes impacts on people and planet plus financial risk [2, 3].
- The CSRD focuses on comprehensive sustainability reporting and aligns with the European Green Deal and GRI. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, CSRD row]])
- In scope for CSRD, EU companies must have more than 1,000 employees and more than EUR 450m net turnover, or non-EU groups with over EUR 450m EU turnover and an EU subsidiary or branch above EUR 200m. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- The revised ESRS adopted on 3 July 2026 will cut mandatory datapoints by over 60% and total datapoints by over 70%. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- ISSB aims to create globally consistent, comparable sustainability standards and integrates financial materiality. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, ISSB row]])
- GRI standards are built on the principle of impact materiality, requiring organizations to report on how their activities affect the economy, environment, and society. ([[raw/Novata_news.pdf#page=3|Novata p.3]])
