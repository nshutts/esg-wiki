# Standards vs Frameworks

ESG frameworks and standards serve distinct purposes in reporting; frameworks provide a broad structure and guiding principles, while standards define specific, prescriptive metrics and methodologies. They are often used together to ensure reports are both structured and rigorous.

## Key details

- ESG frameworks are broad and provide a contextual “frame” on how to disclose material topics in reporting, offering a high-level structure and guiding principles on data presentation. ([[raw/Novata_news.pdf#page=5|Novata p.5]])
- ESG standards are specific and prescriptive, defining the exact metrics and methodologies that must be disclosed. ([[raw/Novata_news.pdf#page=5|Novata p.5]])
- Frameworks define the themes, objectives, and scope of sustainability reporting, guiding companies on what topics to address, the data to collect, and how reporting should be presented, and are largely voluntary. ([[raw/Novata_news.pdf#page=3|Novata p.3]])
- Standards set specific and detailed technical requirements on how sustainability data should be measured, reported, and verified, defining the exact methodology and metrics companies must use. ([[raw/Novata_news.pdf#page=1|Novata pp.1-2]])
- Frameworks are applied to shape the structure of the report, while standards ensure that the reported data is rigorous, consistent, and comparable. ([[raw/Novata_news.pdf#page=5|Novata p.5]])
- Frameworks are largely voluntary, with widely accepted examples including the Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosure (TCFD), the Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP), or the UN’s Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). ([[raw/Novata_news.pdf#page=3|Novata p.3]])
- Standards provide supplementary guidance on reporting language, format, and instructions for applying the standard based on unique business characteristics. ([[raw/Novata_news.pdf#page=1|Novata pp.1-2]])
