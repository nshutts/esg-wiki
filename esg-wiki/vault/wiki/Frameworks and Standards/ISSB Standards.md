# ISSB Standards

The ISSB Standards (IFRS S1 and S2) are investor-focused global baseline standards from the IFRS Foundation designed to help investors and capital markets assess and disclose sustainability-related risks and opportunities. They cover general sustainability-related financial information (S1) and climate-related disclosures (S2).

## Key details

- S1 covers general sustainability-related financial information. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- S2 covers climate-related disclosures. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- Both standards use the four TCFD pillars: governance, strategy, risk management, metrics and targets. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- Both standards require Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions and climate scenario analysis. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- Both standards apply a financial materiality lens. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- As of April 2026, 28 jurisdictions had adopted the standards on a voluntary or mandatory basis, and 12 more were planned to adopt them. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- Australia, Japan, the UK and Brazil have issued ISSB-based standards. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- Enforcement of the standards is done by the regulators that adopt them, not by the ISSB itself. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])

## Related notes

- [[TCFD]]: IFRS S1 and S2 use the four TCFD pillars and absorbed the TCFD recommendations after it disbanded.
- [[SASB Standards]]: SASB is part of the IFRS Foundation, and the ISSB builds on its industry-specific approach.
- [[Double Materiality]]: The ISSB applies a financial materiality lens, while CSRD requires double materiality.
- [[Australia AASB S2]]: Australia's mandatory climate standard is based on IFRS S2.
- [[California SB 253 and SB 261]]: SB 261 climate risk reports may follow TCFD or ISSB.
