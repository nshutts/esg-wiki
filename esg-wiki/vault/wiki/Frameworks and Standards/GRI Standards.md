# GRI Standards

GRI Standards are the most widely used impact-focused framework for sustainability reporting, designed for all stakeholders rather than just investors. They are built on the principle of impact materiality, requiring organizations to report on how their activities affect the economy, environment, and society.

## Key details

- GRI Standards are known as the most widely used standards for sustainability reporting, with over 70% of leading companies globally adopting it as a primary framework for disclosing ESG impact. ([[raw/Novata_news.pdf#page=3|Novata p.3]])
- The GRI Standards are built on the principle of impact materiality, requiring organizations to report on how their activities affect the economy, environment, and society. ([[raw/Novata_news.pdf#page=3|Novata p.3]])
- Components of the GRI Standards include Universal Standards (GRI 1 to 3), 30+ topic standards, and sector standards. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- New GRI 102 (Climate Change) and GRI 103 (Energy) apply to reports published from 1 January 2027, which add transition plan, just transition, and carbon credit disclosures. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- The GRI Standards are voluntary for any organization of any size or sector. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- Claiming "in accordance with GRI" requires a content index and notification to GRI; ESRS is designed to be interoperable with it. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- The GRI framework focuses on the wider impact of sustainability on stakeholders, beyond financial materiality. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, GRI row]])
