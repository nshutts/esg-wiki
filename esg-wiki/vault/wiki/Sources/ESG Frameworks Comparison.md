# ESG Frameworks Comparison

This note compares various ESG frameworks, highlighting their focus areas, scope, and reporting requirements. It is important because these frameworks guide corporate sustainability reporting and disclosure practices.

## Key details

- SASB focuses on industry-specific ESG performance and is voluntary but often integrated into financial disclosures. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, SASB row]])
- SBTi focuses on climate goals and GHG emissions reductions, requiring companies to commit to science-based emissions reduction targets. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, SBTi row]])
- CSRD requires comprehensive sustainability reporting for large EU companies, with a phased approach starting in 2024, and integrates double materiality. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, CSRD row]])
- TCFD focuses on climate-related financial risk and emphasizes financial impact of climate risks through scenario analysis. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, TCFD row]])
- GRI focuses on broad sustainability impacts on stakeholders beyond financial materiality and has sector-specific standards available. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, GRI row]])
- CDP focuses on environmental impact, particularly carbon emissions, and is used by investors and governments. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, CDP row]])
- ISSB aims to create globally consistent, comparable sustainability standards and integrates financial materiality. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table, ISSB row]])
- SASB focuses on industry-specific guidance, while SBTi focuses on emissions targets, with SASB emphasizing financial materiality and SBTi emphasizing climate science. ([[raw/ESG_Frameworks.docx|Comparison table]])
