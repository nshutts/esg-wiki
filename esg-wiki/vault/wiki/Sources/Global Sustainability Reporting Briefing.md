# Global Sustainability Reporting Briefing

This briefing outlines various global sustainability reporting frameworks, noting that while many are voluntary, they form the basis for mandatory regimes. These frameworks cover investor-focused standards (ISSB), stakeholder-focused standards (GRI), emissions accounting (GHG Protocol), and jurisdiction-specific regulations (CSRD, EU Taxonomy, California laws).

## Key details

- ISSB Standards (IFRS S1 and S2) cover general sustainability-related financial information (S1) and climate information (S2), using the four TCFD pillars, requiring Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions, and applying a financial materiality lens. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- As of April 2026, 28 jurisdictions had adopted ISSB Standards voluntarily or mandatorily, with 12 more planned to adopt. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- GRI Standards are the most widely used impact-focused framework, built on impact materiality and covering Universal Standards (GRI 1 to 3) and 30+ topic standards. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- New GRI 102 (Climate Change) and GRI 103 (Energy) apply to reports published from 1 January 2027, adding transition plan, just transition, and carbon credit disclosures. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing p.1]])
- The GHG Protocol is the accounting rulebook for emissions, including the Corporate Standard, Scope 2 Guidance, and Scope 3 Standard (15 value chain categories). ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=1|Briefing pp.1-2]])
- The EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) and ESRS are mandatory reporting laws for EU companies with more than 1,000 employees and more than EUR 450m net turnover. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- The EU Taxonomy Regulation classifies economic activities as environmentally sustainable based on six environmental objectives, requiring companies to report the share of turnover, CapEx, and OpEx that is eligible and aligned. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=2|Briefing p.2]])
- California SB 253 requires annual Scope 1 and 2 emissions reporting from 2026 and Scope 3 from 2027, measured under the GHG Protocol, with third-party assurance phasing from limited to reasonable. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=3|Briefing pp.3-4]])
- The Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) requires companies with more than 5,000 employees and EUR 1.5bn net worldwide turnover to identify, prevent, and remediate adverse human rights and environmental impacts by 26 July 2029. ([[raw/Global_Sustainability_Reporting_Standards_Executive_Briefing.pdf#page=3|Briefing p.3]])
